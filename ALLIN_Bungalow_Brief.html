What Is Polymarket

Polymarket is a prediction market. Anyone with a crypto wallet can bet real money on real-world outcomes: elections, military strikes, government decisions, the death of a head of state. It is not a stock exchange and not technically a casino, which is how it has avoided most regulation. It was founded in 2020 by Shayne Coplan, who was 22 years old at the time, and is registered in Panama.

The concept is old. Prediction markets have existed in academic and limited commercial forms since the 1990s. The theory: people betting real money on an outcome produce more accurate forecasts than any poll or model. The problem: the same mechanism that makes them accurate makes them a tool for anyone who already knows the answer.

The platform has grown beyond anything its critics imagined. In early 2025 it processed roughly $1.2 billion a month in bets. By March 2026 it processed $10.5 billion in a single month. Intercontinental Exchange, the company that owns the New York Stock Exchange, completed a $2 billion investment in Polymarket in March 2026. The NYSE owner is now inside Polymarket. The platform is currently in talks to raise $400 million more at a $15 billion valuation.

History of the Ban

In 2022, the CFTC fined Polymarket $1.4 million for operating an unregistered derivatives exchange and required it to stop serving US users. Polymarket did not shut down. It moved offshore to Panama and kept building. A US user with a VPN and a crypto wallet can access it in under five minutes. The ban accomplished one thing: it put the platform outside US regulatory reach while doing almost nothing to stop American participation.

Kalshi is the US-registered version, a CFTC-supervised prediction market that launched after the offshore model proved the demand. Kalshi operates under US rules. Polymarket does not. The serious money and the risks all live offshore.

Here is the sequence that explains everything. November 2024: the FBI raided Coplan's apartment and took his phone two weeks before the presidential election. He was never charged. July 2025: the Trump administration's DOJ and CFTC dropped all investigations into Polymarket and Coplan with no charges filed. August 2025: Donald Trump Jr.'s firm invested double-digit millions in Polymarket and Trump Jr. joined its advisory board. He was already on Kalshi's advisory board. December 2025: Polymarket was cleared to re-enter the US market. The platform that the outgoing administration had under investigation is now operating freely, backed by the president's son, with the son's former business associate running the agency that regulates it.

The Worst Case Scenario

What is already happening: US military personnel with advance knowledge of operations are placing bets before those operations execute and collecting when they succeed. Master Sergeant Gannon Ken Van Dyke, a US Army Special Forces operator, was federally indicted for doing this. He participated in planning "Operation Absolute Resolve," the classified mission that captured Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro on January 3, 2026. Starting December 26, 2025, he placed 13 bets on Maduro-related markets, invested $33,034, and collected $409,881. He was charged April 23, 2026. He pleaded not guilty. Trial: December 7.

That same month: a Google information security engineer named Michele Spagnuolo was charged with using confidential internal Google search data to bet that singer d4vd would be the most searched person of 2025. He wagered $2.7 million and made $1.2 million. Trading under the handle "AlphaRaccoon," using a crypto mixer to hide his tracks. He faces up to 50 years across three charges. The platform has also referred nearly 100 wallets to law enforcement for suspicious activity. Campaign staffers have admitted anonymously to using internal polling data to bet on their own candidates. Nobody has been charged for that. In April 2026, the White House issued an internal email warning its own staff not to place prediction market bets related to Iran or other government actions. The White House knew.

What could happen next: Polymarket odds are broadcast in real time on CNN and Reuters. When a cluster of wallets bets heavily that a US strike will occur in the next 48 hours, every adversary monitoring the platform sees that signal. The element of surprise is gone before the planes leave the runway.

The question nobody wants to say out loud: if you have the ability to influence an event, and a financial stake in a specific outcome, you have both means and motive to make it happen. DARPA identified this in 2003 and killed its own program because of it. The same concept is now running commercially, offshore, with hundreds of millions of dollars in bets on US government actions every month.

The DARPA Moment

In 2003, DARPA proposed a prediction market called the Policy Analysis Market to gather intelligence on Middle East political instability. Senators Ron Wyden and Byron Dorgan took the Senate floor and called it a "terrorism futures market," asking one question: what happens if a terrorist bets on the attack he is planning? DARPA canceled the program within 48 hours.

The concept did not die. It moved offshore, evolved through two decades of academic research and crypto infrastructure, and became Polymarket. An investigative series published in June 2026 by journalists Whitney Webb and Mark Goodwin argues that Polymarket is not a coincidence. Their reporting suggests Peter Thiel deliberately revived the PAM concept as a private, offshore, unaccountable entity, and that Coplan communicated with architects of the original DARPA program. Nobody has asked the question from the Senate floor this time. Several have asked it in writing. Nobody has gotten a straight answer.

The Iran Example

Before a US military strike on Iran, Polymarket odds shifted sharply. Nine interconnected anonymous wallets placed bets across more than 80 Iran-related markets and achieved a 98% win rate, collecting $2.4 million. Every account was opened within days of the first US assault. One trader, using the handle "Magamyman," placed an $87,000 bet on a US strike 71 minutes before news broke publicly, then followed it with a bet on Supreme Leader Khamenei's death. Total winnings: $553,000. The accounts were identified and analyzed by blockchain analytics firm Bubblemaps, whose findings were presented to 60 Minutes.

Data scientist Michelle Kendler-Kretsch had been tracking the same market in real time, running probability models in her notebook, watching the odds move. She was awake at 4am with her one-year-old daughter when the news confirmed what she had been watching on her screen. Two Israeli citizens were arrested by Shin Bet for using classified information about Israeli strikes on Iran to place Polymarket bets. They are the first people arrested anywhere for prediction market insider trading using military secrets.

"The element of surprise is everything. And you've ruined that on Polymarket?"

Michelle Kendler-Kretsch  ·  Data Scientist  ·  60 Minutes

The Ceasefire, the Pardons, the Teleprompter

On April 7, 2026, more than 50 newly created Polymarket wallets placed bets on a US-Iran ceasefire in the hours and minutes before President Trump announced one. One account turned $13,200 into $467,515. Every account had zero prior trading history. Bloomberg identified the cluster and traced the timing. Senator Blumenthal wrote directly to Coplan demanding answers.

Separately, a Polymarket trader placed roughly $64,000 in bets on pre-emptive pardons for Jim Biden, Liz Cheney, Adam Schiff, and Hunter Biden, netting $316,343. Joshua Mitts, a Columbia Law School professor who advises the DOJ on insider trading cases, reviewed the trades and said the odds of this happening by chance are "virtually zero."

On July 16, four days before this brief was updated, CNBC and NPR reported that Gabriel Perez, Donald Trump's personal teleprompter operator since 2016, had allegedly used advance knowledge of presidential speeches to bet on Kalshi's "mention markets," which pay out based on whether specific words appear in a given speech. He made over $100,000 across 12 speeches. Kalshi froze his account, referred the matter to the CFTC, and the White House placed Perez on unpaid leave. Federal prosecutors in Manhattan reviewed the case and declined to bring criminal charges. The CFTC entered settlement talks.

Bloomberg's analysis of Polymarket trade data flagged $200 million in suspicious transactions in the first half of 2026 alone. Researchers at Columbia Law School and the University of Haifa analyzed 210,000 suspicious transactions since 2024 and found $143 million in anomalous profits for informed traders.

Elections

Prediction markets now cover 96% of US primary races heading into the midterms. The same platform that broadcast the Iran strike in advance runs markets on every congressional seat. A single $1,000 bet on a candidate triggers a cascade of automated bots that follow large money moves, making a candidate appear more likely to win without any actual shift in voter support. None of that counts toward campaign finance limits. Nothing stops a foreign government from doing it. The Atlantic Council published a formal paper this year calling prediction markets "a new vector for foreign influence" and urging homeland security authorities to treat them as an election threat.

Kalshi launched more than 150 investigations into insider trading from political campaigns in the first quarter of 2026 alone. It blocked more than 100 trades and referred at least 20 cases to law enforcement. Campaign staffers are still attempting to bet. George Santos bet against his own attendance at Trump's State of the Union address after publicly announcing he would attend. A delayed flight kept him away. He had already bet on his own absence. The DOJ opened a probe.

Romania

In Romania's 2024 presidential election, $371 million was wagered on Polymarket. The figure dwarfed the country's entire political advertising market. Romania banned Polymarket outright. Romania then had its election result cancelled by its own constitutional court due to documented foreign interference. It held a repeat election in 2025. Polymarket ran markets on that election too. Romania is the only country in the world that has cancelled a presidential election because of documented digital interference. It is also the only country that has explicitly banned a prediction market platform because of what it did to an election. One expert told me on the record, for the first time publicly, that Romania got it right and the markets should be banned. That is her personal position, separate from her organization's official stance. She made that distinction explicitly when she spoke to me.

Money Laundering

In Ireland's 2024 Dublin Central by-election, more than $1 million was wagered on Polymarket. The Irish Times investigated and surfaced the name Gerry Hutch, a figure with documented connections to organized crime. The investigation identified patterns consistent with wash trading: money moving between wallets controlled by the same actor to simulate market activity. Irish Finance Minister Simon Harris called it "a Wild West of unregulated betting" and asked authorities to investigate. On July 19, three days before this brief was updated, the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland formally threatened Polymarket with High Court proceedings if it does not comply with Irish licensing requirements. Polymarket responded by blocking Ireland entirely. France, Spain, and the Czech Republic have ordered ISPs to block Polymarket. Indonesia banned it on May 25. India classified it as prohibited online money gaming effective May 1. Forty-plus countries now block the platform.

The Facebook parallel keeps coming up. Zuckerberg built a connection platform. The outcome in Myanmar was genocide, because he connected people who wanted to find each other for that purpose. The technology outran the capacity to regulate it. Prediction markets are the same dynamic applied to war, elections, and government decisions, with one difference: prediction markets are not an accidental side effect. They are specifically designed to put money on these outcomes.

How It Works: Click by Click

  1. Someone with insider knowledge places a bet through an anonymous crypto wallet on an offshore platform the US government cannot touch.
  2. The blockchain is public. Bots and sophisticated traders monitor it in real time, see the unusual movement, and pile on.
  3. The market shifts, broadcasting the insider signal to everyone watching, including foreign adversaries.
  4. When the event resolves, the insider collects. The bots collect. The platform takes its fee. The government learns about it months later, through a journalist or an indictment.
  5. Nobody goes to jail. The CFTC has one commissioner. Enforcement dropped from 58 cases in 2024 to 13 in 2025. The wallets are anonymous. The platform is offshore. There is no explicit statute making any of this illegal. The CFTC is currently suing nine states to keep it that way. December 7 will be the first time a US court has to answer the question of whether any of it was ever criminal.

Four More Things

Selling positions. You do not have to wait for an event to happen to profit. If you know a US strike is coming, you buy a "yes" position when the odds are 10% and the price is cheap. As insiders and bots move the market, the price of your position rises. You sell at 60% before the strike happens and pocket the gain. The event never has to occur. Blockchain analysts documented this exact pattern across the nine Iran-linked accounts that made $2.4 million. They never held their positions to resolution. They sold into the movement they created.

Market manipulation. The price on Polymarket is set purely by bettors, not by any external model. One large bet moves the price. That movement triggers bots that move it further. Fourteen of the twenty most profitable wallets on Polymarket's public leaderboard are bots. They exploit the latency between Polymarket and Kalshi, finding persistent arbitrage edges of 1 to 5 percent, faster than any human can react. You do not need to know the outcome. You need capital and no conscience.

Are people incentivizing outcomes? Can those outcomes kill people? The Van Dyke indictment does not allege he manufactured the Maduro operation. He knew about it and bet on it. But the market structure creates a financial incentive to make specific events happen. At sufficient scale, with sufficient access to the right levers, that is not a theoretical concern. DARPA identified it in 2003 and killed its own program because of it. Nobody killed Polymarket. The experts I have spoken to believe the scenario is possible. None of them will say it has happened yet. None of them will rule it out.

Who decides what you can bet on? The platform does. Before 2000, the CFTC required pre-approval of every new derivatives contract. The Commodity Futures Modernization Act eliminated that and replaced it with self certification: platforms declare that their own contracts meet legal standards. Kalshi listed a contract on the death of Iran's Supreme Leader. It was removed after the fact. The CFTC proposed a rule on June 10, 2026 that would formally ban war, terrorism, and assassination contracts. The public comment period closes July 27. That is five days from now. There is no guarantee the rule survives. The CFTC chair who proposed it came from a law firm that represented the platforms the rule would restrict.
