June 21, 2026

The Fake Winners

The Wall Street Journal published a visual investigation revealing that Polymarket had paid mostly college-age creators to film fake winning trades on dummy replica websites designed to look identical to the live platform. One counterfeit domain, poiymarket.com, was so close to the real URL that only a capital I gave it away.

Matthew Modabber paid creators via personal PayPal. That account sent over $2.5M to 800+ people total.

Creators were paid between $2,000 and $3,000 per month and instructed not to disclose their relationship with Polymarket. They were paid only when at least 60 percent of their audience was based in the United States. That condition was deliberate: Polymarket has been barred from serving American users since a 2022 CFTC settlement.

Polymarket employed a marketing firm called Virality to manage an army of clippers: teenagers, mostly in Asia, who reposted creator content on freshly created accounts for $1 per 1,000 views. Internal chat logs from nearly 20,000 messages showed Virality instructing clippers to make reposts feel spontaneous and authentically personal and to remove any mention of Polymarket from their usernames.

The Adin Ross Deal

Adin Ross, a 25-year-old creator with 19 million followers, has a multimillion-dollar deal with Polymarket and averages a half hour per week on his livestream scrolling through Polymarket and discussing potential trades. In at least five videos, Ross identified on camera how he could use inside information to win on the platform. Polymarket and Virality targeted his videos for promotion. Ross called it information arbitrage.

The same week, TikTok began restricting accounts identified in the Journal's investigation. YouTube said creators and brands must comply with legal obligations. Meta said it could not confirm whether the content violated its policies without independently verifying the payments.

The Human Story

50 People Lost the Bet George Makihara Faked Winning

On Polymarket's actual site, public data shows more than 50 real accounts placed the Trump McDonald's bet in January 2026. George Makihara's videos showed him winning $100,000 on that position. The videos were fake. The bet was filmed on a counterfeit website.

All 50 accounts lost.

These are ordinary people who watched what looked like a college student winning $100,000 on a simple bet, decided to place the same wager on the real platform, and lost real money on a position that had been manufactured to look like free money. They are the human consequence of a marketing campaign the company's CEO told his growth team to make impossible to ignore.

June 25, 2026

The $3.1 Million Hack

A third-party vendor that supplies frontend code to Polymarket was compromised. Attackers injected a malicious script into the page users see in their browser, tricking users into signing unauthorized transactions. On-chain tracking by PeckShield and Bubblemaps confirmed approximately $3.1 million drained from 11 to 15 wallets. The stolen funds were converted to roughly 1,893 ETH and moved through attacker-controlled addresses.

Polymarket pledged full refunds to all affected users and said it had contained and removed the malicious code. The hack arrived four days after the fake winner story dropped and one day before the triple regulatory escalation.

The Named Victim

A user known as Ash posted on X in real time as his wallet was being drained, saying he had no idea what was happening. He shared his wallet address and the attacker's address publicly, giving on-chain investigators the data to begin tracking. He is the only named victim in public reporting. Ten others lost funds without being publicly identified.

June 26, 2026

Three Escalations. One Day.

On the same day, three separate institutions moved against Polymarket.

CFTC
Expanded Federal Probe
Bloomberg reports the CFTC is conducting a broad probe into Polymarket's operations and social media activity, expanding beyond the prior investigation that was closed in July 2025 with no charges. The CFTC chair overseeing this probe is Michael Selig, whose ethics recusal from his former crypto clients expires mid-2026.
Federal Lawsuit
Coplan + Modabber Named Personally
Vaca Daffan Law files on behalf of the National Association of Consumer Advocates. Defendants: Blockratize (parent company), CEO Shayne Coplan, and CMO Matthew Modabber personally. Allegation: a sweeping and flagrantly deceptive marketing campaign targeting college-aged individuals.
Senate
Curtis + Schiff Joint Letter
Senators John Curtis (R-UT) and Adam Schiff (D-CA) write jointly to CFTC Chairman Michael Selig calling the allegations deeply troubling and demanding a written response by July 10, 2026. Curtis and Schiff are the same bipartisan pair who introduced legislation targeting sports wagers on CFTC-regulated platforms.

Through all three escalations, Polymarket reported revenue crossing one billion dollars.

The Context That Makes This Matter

The CFTC that is now probing Polymarket is the same CFTC that cleared it in July 2025. The chair running this probe previously represented Polymarket-adjacent clients at Willkie Farr. Donald Trump Jr. holds equity in Polymarket and sits on its advisory board. He is simultaneously a paid strategic adviser to Kalshi, Polymarket's primary regulated rival. Trump personally called state politicians who want to regulate prediction markets "SCUM" in a Truth Social post. The CFTC under Selig has filed more than half a dozen lawsuits to stop states from regulating or taxing prediction markets. The regulatory pressure converging on June 26 is landing on a platform that sits at the center of the Trump financial network.
