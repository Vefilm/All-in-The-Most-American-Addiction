The Cast
June 2026  ·  VEFILM

These are not talking heads. They are people inside a story that has not ended. Each character has an arc that closes — or doesn't — by December 7, 2026, when the first prediction market insider trading trial in US history begins. The wallets are still active. The legislative window is open. We are filming now.

The Characters
Five people. One pattern. No name for the crime yet.

Each of these characters found the same thing from a different direction. A blockchain analyst in Paris. A financial crime investigator who saw it happen first in the Congo. A commodities attorney who named the mechanism on national television. A political scientist whose dataset found the anomaly for him. A legislative watchdog who knows which senator actually wants to fix it and which one wants a press release. We follow all of them.

The Ghost
Deebs
Bubblemaps · Paris
Anonymous
"The pattern is clear. The question is whether anyone in power actually wants to see it."
■ On Camera Confirmed

He found the network nobody was looking for. Working under a pseudonym from Paris, Deebs is a blockchain analyst at Bubblemaps who mapped a cluster of nine coordinated wallets placing precise bets on US military outcomes minutes before they happened. He does not use his real name. His face is sometimes obscured. His location is not confirmed. But his data is on-chain, timestamped, and irrefutable.

He found something that should have been front-page news across America. Nobody covered it. He published it anyway. The wallets kept betting. We follow Deebs as the network he identified continues operating in real time, as the institutions that should respond stay silent, and as the story he told on a blockchain explorer slowly becomes impossible to ignore.

He is the most visually compelling character in the series: screens, cursor, anonymity, and receipts. The espionage quality is not a stylistic choice. It is the situation.

His Arc
Deebs found the evidence before anyone was investigating. We follow him as the world catches up to what he already knows — and as he decides how visible to become.
The Veteran
Michael Hornsby
ACDC Co-Director
Paris, France
"Insider information is a feature on prediction markets. That is not a bug. That is the point."
■ On Camera Confirmed

He has done this before. In 2021, Hornsby co-directed the Congo Hold-Up investigation, exposing a $1.5 billion theft from the Central Bank of the Democratic Republic of Congo orchestrated through shell companies and banking insiders. He has watched financial crime meet political power and win. He knows how it ends when nobody stops it.

Now he is watching the same machine reassemble in the United States wearing a different suit. He operates from Paris, draws connections across continents, and speaks in precise, deliberate sentences. When we got on a call with him, he drew the line from Central Africa to Washington DC without being asked. He called the dynamic "regulatory capture versus regulatory deployment" and explained the difference in two minutes flat.

We follow Hornsby as the pattern he has spent his career studying arrives on American soil and the institutions that should respond begin to look the other way. He is the character who has seen the ending before.

His Arc
Hornsby is the historical mirror. He bridges the Congo and the US. We follow him as the parallel either holds or breaks — and as he reckons with what it means that it held.
The Lawyer
David Kovel
Kirby McInerney LLP
New York
"Any trader right now is highly suspicious. Whoever placed that trade appeared to know the announcement was coming."
■ On Camera Confirmed

David Kovel is the recipient of the largest CFTC whistleblower award in history. He has spent his career at the intersection of commodities law and financial fraud. He knows what the crime is. He named the mechanism on record to 60 Minutes in May 2026 when no one else would. He knows why nobody has been prosecuted for the $800 million oil trade placed fifteen minutes before Trump's Iran announcement moved the market. He knows what the CFTC can do — and what it has chosen not to.

We follow Kovel as the Van Dyke trial approaches December 7 and as the legal system either confronts what it knows or decides to look away. He is the character who can tell us whether what we are watching is a failure of law or a decision not to enforce it. That distinction is the whole series.

His Arc
Kovel named the crime on national television. The question his arc answers: does naming it change anything? December 7 is his deadline too.
The Scientist
David Szakonyi
George Washington University
Washington DC
"52% of longshot bets on military outcomes won. The baseline is 14%. That is not a theory. That is a number."
○ In Development

Szakonyi is an associate professor at George Washington University and co-author of the ACDC report that formally documented the 52% longshot win rate in military prediction markets across more than 435,000 settled markets. He did not set out to find insider trading. He set out to analyze a dataset. The dataset found it for him.

He published the number in April 2026. Congress received it. Some senators wrote letters. Others said nothing. We follow Szakonyi through the institutional response to his research: who calls him, who sends the letter, who does not call back, and what the silence says about where this ends. He is the character who handed Washington proof and now has to watch what Washington does with it.

His Arc
The scientist published the proof. His arc is the gap between what the data says and what the government does. That gap is closing or widening in real time.
The Fixer
Janice Luong
Project on Government Oversight
Washington DC
"There is real legislation and there is a press release. Right now Washington is producing a lot of press releases."
○ In Development

Janice Luong works at the Project on Government Oversight, the nonprofit that has been in the room when congressional staff draft legislation and when it gets watered down. She knows what real regulation of prediction markets would look like. She knows what is actually moving on the Hill and what is theater. She knows the difference between a senator who wants to fix this and a senator who wants a press release about wanting to fix this.

We follow Luong through the legislative window that is open now and will not stay open. After the Van Dyke trial on December 7, the public attention moves on and the window closes. She is the character who is trying to get something real through before that happens. We are filming her while there is still something to film.

Her Arc
Luong is the legislative clock. She either gets something real through before December or she doesn't. Either ending is a story about American governance.
■ The Clock

December 7, 2026. USA v. Van Dyke. The first prediction market insider trading trial in US history begins in the Southern District of New York. Every character in this series has something at stake in that outcome. The wallets are still active. The $800 million oil trade has not been investigated. The CFTC has not responded to Elizabeth Warren's letter. The legislative window has not closed yet. This is why we are making this now.
