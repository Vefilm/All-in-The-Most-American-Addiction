The Cold Open
November 5, 2024. The presidential election is called, the largest single day in prediction market history. Eight days later, the FBI seizes the phone of Polymarket's CEO. Then the new administration takes office, the case is quietly dropped, no charges, no explanation, and a month later the president's son's fund invests in the platform.
The CEO was never touched. This is where the film begins.

It looks like harmless fun, a bet on an election or a game. Follow the money and it becomes something else. The platforms are offshore by design. The agency that is supposed to police them has been turned into an instrument that protects them. And the people who cracked this are spread across Paris, Washington and New York, each holding one piece. The film enters through the most American thing there is, the bet, and lands on the family at the top.

The Owners

You do not have to place a bet to win.
You have to own the house.

This is the part everyone tiptoes around. These markets are not just tolerated at the top. They are wired into it.

Donald Trump Jr. holds equity. He does not need to place a single insider bet.

Insider activity creates volume. Volume creates value. Value accrues to whoever owns the platform. No bet, no crime, entirely legal under the law as it stands. The family's crypto venture, World Liberty Financial, is tied in, and Truth Predict, a prediction market built into the MAGA media ecosystem, points the same audience at the same machine. This is the version no individual prosecution can reach, which is why blanket bans matter more than one soldier's trial.

On traders who hold inside information: "having an edge to the market is a good thing."

Shayne Coplan, Polymarket CEO · 60 Minutes, 2026
And the referee now plays for one team. Not regulatory capture. Regulatory deployment.

Under Chairman Michael Selig, the CFTC has filed briefs and lawsuits siding with the platforms against the states, and when New York sued Kalshi as illegal gambling, the agency went to court to stop New York. It has gutted its own enforcement, from 96 actions in 2023 to 13 in 2025, and let the Chicago enforcement division fall from twenty trial lawyers to zero. Selig disbanded the technology advisory committee and replaced it with a council of thirty five executives drawn from the very industry he regulates. The Federal Trade Commission, Wall Street money, and the platforms all pull the same direction. The house and the referee are now the same people.

Same Structure, Different Suit

This has happened before. In the Congo.

In 2021 a coalition of nineteen outlets published the Congo Hold Up, the largest financial leak in African history, showing how a president's family drained a country through a bank they controlled while the central bank looked away. The team that co authored it, ACDC, found the same shape operating inside the United States. Michael Hornsby puts it in four words. Same structure, different suit.

It is the same setup in both countries.

A regulator that looks the other way. Anonymous accounts that move the money out of sight. And it ends up with the family at the top. In the Congo it was shell companies and a bank the family controlled. Here it is anonymous wallets, a captured CFTC, and the family's own crypto fund. The bet on the phone is where the money goes in. The family at the top is where it comes out.

The Precedent

The government already killed this once.

In 2003 the Pentagon built a market that let people bet on assassinations and terror attacks, the Policy Analysis Market. Congress killed it in a day, called it grotesque, and shut it down. Twenty three years later the same idea is a private business worth tens of billions, and the government that once killed it now protects it.

A market that pays you for knowing what is about to happen becomes a market that pays you for making it happen. When the people who can move troops, ships and missiles can also hold the winning position, the incentive no longer points toward peace.

We are not claiming a bet pulled a trigger. We are showing that the structure now rewards anyone who can. That is the line Congress drew in 2003, and it is open again.
The Map

This is not only a Washington story. It runs across borders.

The market itself is offshore, the money moves everywhere, and the investigators who cracked it are sitting in different cities on different continents, each holding one piece. The Congo is where this exact method was born, before the same tools were ever turned on Polymarket.

Three field cities where the investigators are, converging on one machine. The trail runs out to the Congo bank scandal that started this method, and to the wars and leaders these markets are quietly betting on.

The scientist who set out to analyze a dataset, and the dataset handed him a crime.

His team found military longshots win about 51.8 percent of the time against roughly 14 percent everywhere else. He says these markets are closer to a Ponzi than a fair bet, and the honest fix is to get rid of them.
